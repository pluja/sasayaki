<p>Sasayaki lets you dictate into any app. You talk, it sends the audio to a speech-to-text server you choose, and types the text where you are. That can be your own Whisper server or any service with an OpenAI-compatible API.</p>

<p><b>Two ways to dictate</b></p>
<ul>
<li>A small bubble shows up when the keyboard opens. Tap it and talk. It stops when you go quiet.</li>
<li>Or use the mic key of keyboards like HeliBoard and FlorisBoard. Sasayaki opens in the keyboard area. This only needs the microphone permission.</li>
</ul>

<p><b>Your servers</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Works with whisper.cpp, faster-whisper, speaches, llama.cpp and many hosted services.</li>
<li>Servers on your home network or tailnet can use plain http. Certificates from your own authority work once installed on the phone.</li>
<li>Audio only goes to the servers you add. No analytics, no ads, no cloud backup.</li>
<li>Recordings with no speech are dropped instead of sent.</li>
<li>It won't dictate into password fields or apps it recognises as banking apps.</li>
</ul>

<p><b>Cleanup (optional)</b></p>
<p>If you add a chat model, Sasayaki tidies up what you said: it drops filler words, applies corrections like "at 8, no, at 9", and fixes punctuation. You choose how much it rewrites or shortens. Without a chat model you get the plain transcript.</p>

<p><b>Also</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Profiles with their own language, models and style. Sasayaki remembers which one you use in each app.</li>
<li>A vocabulary list so names and jargon come out right.</li>
<li>Replacement rules for words your speech model keeps getting wrong.</li>
<li>History of your dictations, kept on the phone. You can copy or retry them, limit it, or turn it off.</li>
<li>Export and import your settings to move to a new phone.</li>
</ul>

<p><b>Permissions</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Microphone: only while you dictate.</li>
<li>Display over other apps and accessibility (bubble only): to show the bubble and type into the field you're using. Accessibility reads that field to put the text at the cursor.</li>
<li>Notifications (optional): shows when the bubble is on, with a button to turn it off.</li>
</ul>

<p>You need a speech-to-text server to use it. The project page lists some that work well.</p>
